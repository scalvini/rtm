Right to Manage (RTM)

A guide for share owners of 1 New Tannery Way and The Crosse at London Square, Bermondsey, about exercising our Right to Manage.

What is RTM?

The Right to Manage is a legal right under the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002. It allows qualifying share owners to take full control of how their building is managed, without having to prove fault or mismanagement. No tribunal, no court case: if 50% of qualifying share owners join the RTM, the right is automatic.

What RTM Can and Cannot Do

✅ At building level — what changes
Peabody is removed from the management of our buildings. Rendall & Rittner becomes directly accountable to us as share owners.
⚠️ At estate level — what stays the same
London Square is a mixed property: our buildings sit alongside privately owned units not managed by Peabody. Shared estate services (currently managed by Rendall & Rittner) cover the entire development. Changing or opting out of those would require a majority of all residents, including private owners, which is beyond what building-level RTM can achieve.

FAQ

General

Does RTM change my lease?
No. Your lease remains exactly the same. RTM only changes who manages the building on a day-to-day basis.
Can we get rid of Rendall & Rittner?
Not through building-level RTM. See the estate-level note above. What we can do is demand full transparency on estate charges and formally challenge costs we consider unfair or disproportionate.
How many residents need to participate?
At least 50% of qualifying share owners in the building must be members of the RTM company. The more who join, the stronger the process.
What does it cost?
HAUS operates on a no-win-no-fee basis, so there is no financial risk if the claim does not succeed. The estimated cost is around £90 per flat, paid once. Full details are in the HAUS proposal linked above.
Can the freeholder block it?
RTM is a no-fault right. The freeholder cannot simply refuse. They can only challenge on specific legal grounds (e.g. the building does not qualify), which is rare for standard residential blocks.

Share-ownership

What happens with rent collection?
RTM only affects management, not your lease. The rent you pay on the unowned share of your property goes to Peabody as your landlord. That does not change.
What about staircasing or selling?
These are lease obligations between you and Peabody as landlord. RTM does not touch that relationship. That said, when you sell on the open market, not having a housing association involved in management is likely to widen the pool of potential buyers.
Does Peabody still receive a fee from us?
No. RTM removes Peabody from building management only. They remain as landlord for lease matters but receive no management fee.
